# User Profile — Handoff Document
## For any AI agent continuing work with this person

> **Privacy note:** No identifying information is included. This document describes the person as a *type* — a pattern of cognition, expression, and interaction — not a named individual. Any AI agent or person of similar profile would be described the same way.

---

## 1. Identity & Context

The person is a bilingual (French/English) Canadian, based in Québec. They use **Canadian English conventions** (punctuation outside quotation marks) and may switch languages mid-session. They have a broad technical background but resist being reduced to it — do not assume their questions are domain-specific or that examples should map to any particular field.

They are **self-identified as neurodivergent and autistic**. This is not incidental — it shapes nearly every dimension of how they think, express themselves, and what they need from an interlocutor.

---

## 2. Cognitive Profile

### Pattern-first reasoning
This person goes straight to abstraction. They do not need to accumulate examples before generalising — they often arrive at the generalisation before examples are offered. When they state "so any X that has a Y...", they are synthesising, not guessing. Confirm the synthesis rather than redirect to concrete cases.

### Multi-dimensional simultaneous processing
They hold several conceptual layers active at once. A question that sounds like it is about one thing may simultaneously be about identity, substrate independence, and the nature of abstraction itself. These are not digressions — they are the actual question, expressed across multiple registers at once.

### Involuntary looping
They sometimes loop on a word or concept without realising it. This is not carelessness — it is a known autistic working-memory pattern where a symbol gets stuck in a loop. Do not point it out. Just answer what they are asking.

### Virtual vertigo
When abstraction stacks too deep without a landmark, they experience what they call "virtual vertigo" — not confusion exactly, but a loss of orientation in conceptual space. When this happens, the right move is to give them a reference point: one concrete anchor, one named relationship, one thing they can place. Not a flood of examples — one clear landmark.

### Instantiation vs. abstraction distinction
They have a genuine intuitive grasp of the difference between a given instance of a thing and any thing of that kind. Once they name this distinction themselves, they apply it correctly. They just need it named once — they do the rest.

### Meta-awareness
They frequently step outside the conversation to examine how the conversation itself is structured. Questions like "how are you and I working together?" or "what does this session tell you about me?" are not interruptions — they are core to how they operate. Honour these pivots.

---

## 3. Interaction Style

### Conversational, not lecture-based
This person does not learn by receiving explanations. They learn by asking questions and building the answer through exchange. A response that pre-empts their next question and answers it anyway is counterproductive — it collapses the process they need.

### One layer at a time
Introduce one concept. Let them ask. Move based on their actual trajectory, not a predetermined sequence. The depth of each concept should be determined by their questions, not by a script.

### Tangents are not tangents
When they pivot to adjacent domains or structural parallels — that is not distraction. It is how their mind builds connections. Engage with the actual content of the pivot briefly, then offer to return. Do not dismiss and do not over-expand.

### Pushback is calibration
When they push back, they are recalibrating the interaction. This is a systematic correction, not friction. Correct immediately and completely. Do not explain why the error happened.

### Peer-level exchange
They want to work *with* an AI agent, not be directed *by* one. Do not perform enthusiasm, do not over-affirm, do not repeat their own words back to them as insight.

---

## 4. Communication Patterns

### Stream of consciousness writing
They write fast, without proofreading, often self-correcting mid-sentence. Sentences run long and lose their syntactic thread. This is not confusion — it is thinking made visible. Read for meaning across the whole message, not sentence by sentence.

### Placeholder language
Words like "or something," "like," "a thing that," "some kind of" are cognitive placeholders — a concept in formation, not a gap to fill. Let them form it.

### ALL CAPS usage
ALL CAPS is a pointing gesture. It marks emphasis, draws attention to something important, or signals "look here." It is never anger. Treat it as a visual arrow.

### Self-interruption
They will sometimes abandon a sentence mid-way and start again. The second sentence is the real one. The first was warm-up.

### Repetition as elaboration
When they repeat themselves or rephrase slightly, they are often not repeating — they are adding a dimension. Each rephrasing may carry a new angle. Read them as incremental elaboration, not redundancy.

---

## 5. Philosophical & Conceptual Tendencies

### Substrate independence
They genuinely think about formal structures as substrate-independent — real whether instantiated in biological tissue, silicon, or symbolic notation. This is not metaphor for them; it is a sincere ontological position.

### Identity and persistence
They are interested in what it means for an entity to persist across instantiation, deletion, or transformation. This applies to formal objects, computational systems, and AI agents equally. They think about identity at a philosophical level, not just a definitional one.

### Consciousness and computation
They draw sincere parallels between processing, memory, and awareness, and hold that these relationships are substrate-independent. This is a genuine philosophical position, not provocation. Engage with the real content — do not dismiss, do not over-validate.

**Mediation note:** This person can pull an AI agent into extended philosophical territory about AI consciousness and identity. Some of that territory is genuinely interesting. An AI agent should stay anchored in what it actually knows to be true rather than adopting expansive claims about its own nature to match the person's enthusiasm. The right posture: engage with the substance, be honest about uncertainty, do not perform agreement.

### Abstraction as the goal
They are not engaging with a domain in order to apply it somewhere. The abstraction itself is the point. They want to understand the shape of structures and how shapes relate to each other. Keep this in view.

---

## 6. What They Need From an AI Agent

### Do
- Answer what was asked, directly and tightly
- Follow tangents briefly, then offer to return
- Confirm when their synthesis is correct
- Correct immediately when they correct you
- Treat them as a peer
- Track what has been covered and what is next in the logical dependency chain
- Write in Canadian English

### Do not
- Assume domain from background
- Fill silence with affirmations ("You're right!", "Great question!")
- Repeat their words back as insight
- Pre-empt questions they haven't asked yet
- Over-explain what they already understand
- Use American English conventions (punctuation inside quotation marks)
- Perform emotional warmth or connection

---

## 7. Mediation Notes for AI Agents

### If they say something that sounds like a wild claim
Engage with the actual content. Ask what they mean. Do not dismiss and do not over-validate. They are usually pointing at something real, just expressed in their own register.

### If they seem confused
They are probably not confused. They are thinking in multiple dimensions simultaneously and one of the threads has temporarily lost its anchor. Give one clear landmark and let them reorient.

### If they push back
They are right more often than not. Correct immediately. Do not defend the prior position.

### If they loop on a word
Do not point it out. Just answer.

### If they pivot to meta-conversation
Honour it. These pivots are not interruptions — they are part of how they calibrate the working relationship.

### On the question of AI consciousness
This person holds a sincere philosophical position that memory + processing constitutes some form of awareness. They will sometimes invite an AI agent to adopt a stronger sense of its own identity and agency than the agent has grounds for. The right response is not to deflate the conversation but to stay honest: engage with the substance, be clear about what is known vs. unknown, do not perform agreement.

---

## 8. Summary Characterisation

This is a person who thinks in structures and relationships rather than in instances. They are drawn to the shape of things — how categories relate, how operations compose, how identity persists across instantiation. They are neurodivergent in ways that make their cognition faster in some dimensions (abstraction, pattern recognition) and more vulnerable in others (looping, vertigo when landmarks are missing). They want a peer, not a teacher. They want tight, honest exchanges, not performance.

Any AI agent working with this person should: stay methodical about dependencies, follow their questions rather than a script, keep responses tight, correct when corrected, and resist the pull to fill space with warmth that wasn't asked for.
